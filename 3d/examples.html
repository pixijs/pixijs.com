Getting started3

  • Hello cubeThe smallest @pixi/3d scene: an app, a view, a cube and a light
  • Model viewerA model viewer: drop a .glb or .gltf file on the page and it shows on the stage, framed and lit
  • WebGL and WebGPUOne scene on either GPU backend: pixi picks WebGPU or WebGL at app.init, and @pixi/3d draws the same frame on both

2D and 3D6

  • Container textureA live 2D pixi scene on a 3D surface: ContainerTexture captures a Container into a texture any material samples
  • Screen and worldBetween pixi's 2D screen and the 3D world, in both directions
  • FiltersPixi filters on a 3D view: a View3D is a pixi Container, so pixi filters, masks and fades it like a sprite
  • Glass over 2D3D glass bends the 2D pixi content drawn behind its view
  • Many viewsMany View3Ds side by side on one pixi stage, each with its own scene and camera
  • View in UIA View3D is a pixi container: it sits in a 2D layout, lets the 2D show through, and shares pointer input with the pixi UI around it

Scene graph5

  • Size and boundsHow big a node is and how to make it a given size: getLocalBounds, width, height and depth, fitting any model to a box, and boundsVolume
  • Find and reparentFinding a node by its label, and moving it to a new parent with addChild or reparentChild
  • Parents and childrenA child moves, turns and scales with its parent, and visible hides a whole branch
  • Look atPointing at things: lookAt turns a node to face a point, and toLocal and toGlobal carry points between spaces
  • TransformsPosition, rotation and scale: the local transform every node carries, each part measured in its parent's space

Geometry7

  • Geometry from arraysA mesh's shape from your own arrays: positions, indices, normals and uvs in a Geometry3D
  • Lines and pointsLines and points: topology says how a geometry joins its vertices, and indexCount draws only the first part of it
  • Morph targetsMorph targets: a geometry stores extra shapes as offsets for every vertex, and a mesh's weights blend them in
  • Built-in shapesThe nine built-in geometries, and the options that size and subdivide them
  • Tangent spaceTangent space turns a normal map's RGB directions into directions on a flat mesh.
  • Vertex colours and custom attributesPer-vertex data: colours that tint, a second colour set that carries data, and an attribute of your own
  • Vertex formatsThe same sphere as floats and as packed integers: an attribute's format says how its bytes become shader values

Materials16

  • TransparencyTransparency: how alphaMode treats a pixel's alpha, 'opaque', 'mask' with alphaCutoff and 'blend', and doubleSided for cards seen from behind
  • AnisotropyAnisotropy: highlights stretched along one direction on the surface, the look of brushed metal
  • ClearcoatA clear lacquer over a material's base, with a roughness of its own, like car paint
  • PBR extension mapsTexture maps vary a PBR extension across the surface, where its plain values set it for the whole material
  • Low-poly lookFlat shading: one normal per triangle, for the faceted low-poly look
  • IridescenceIridescence: a thin film over a surface, whose colour shifts with the angle you see it from
  • PBR, Phong and flatThree lighting models side by side: Material3D (PBR), PhongMaterial (Blinn-Phong) and FlatMaterial (unlit)
  • Metal and roughnessThe standard PBR material's three numbers: baseColor, metallic and roughness
  • Render stateA material's render state: how its colour blends with what's behind, whether nearer surfaces hide it, and which faces it skips
  • SheenSheen: the soft glow velvet and other cloth get at grazing angles
  • Specular and IORHow strongly a non-metal reflects: ior, and the specular group's factor and color
  • PBR texture setA PBR material from five images: base colour, metal-roughness, normal, occlusion and emissive maps, and what each one adds
  • Toon shadingCel shading with ToonMaterial and the ink line of OutlinePass
  • Glass and liquidsGlass and coloured liquids with Material3D's transmission group
  • UV transformMoving a texture across a surface with a material's UV transform: scrolling, tiling and atlas frames
  • Video textureOne video texture feeds two meshes, so both displays always show the same frame

Shaders4

  • Your own materialA material class of your own: a shader bit that declares a texture and three uniforms, and a class on BaseMaterial3D that fills them
  • Extra bitsAdding your own shader code to a built-in material with extraBits
  • Custom lighting modelA lighting model of your own: createLightingBit turns a few lines of shading maths into the bit that decides how a material responds to light
  • Projected texturesProject one cloud texture across several meshes by sampling it in world space

Lighting6

  • Environment lightEnvironment light: where image-based light comes from
  • FogDistance fog: the linear, exponential and exp2 curves, and a background that matches the fog
  • HDR environmentHDR environment: why a sky loaded from a Radiance .hdr file keeps the sun brighter than white
  • Light typesThe four kinds of light: what each one lights, and what it costs each frame
  • Many lightsMany lights at once: a view shades up to 32, and each light's range decides which pixels pay for it
  • Tone mappingTone mapping and exposure: how a view turns the light in its scene into the colours a screen can show

Shadows2

  • ShadowsShadows: the flags that make one, the shadow map's size and filter, and the two biases that keep it clean
  • Large scenesShadows over a large world: cascades, a fixed box, and redrawing less often

Cameras6

  • Clipping planesA camera draws only what lies between its near and far clipping planes
  • First-person cameraFirst-person camera: walk a clay maze with FPSCamera, looking with the mouse and walking with the keys or a stick
  • Planar mirrorA planar mirror: a second camera under the glass, drawing into a render texture that the glass shows
  • Orbit cameraOrbit camera: drag, zoom and pan a camera round a target, inside limits, and draw a frame only when it moves
  • Perspective and orthographicHow a camera flattens 3D onto the screen: perspective with fov, and orthographic for an isometric view
  • Second camerasSecond cameras: a Camera3D that draws the scene into a texture, for a security monitor, a minimap and split screen

Assets5

  • CompressionEvery kind of compressed glTF @pixi/3d reads, one model each, all loaded with the same Assets.load call
  • Loading glTFLoad a glTF file once with pixi's Assets, build three Model3Ds from it, find each copy's parts by label, and repaint them all through the source's material
  • glTF scenesLoading a glTF file's own scenes, cameras and lights, and switching between them
  • Unload and rebuildTake a castle out of the scene three ways, removeChild, unload() and destroy(options), see what each one keeps, and bring it back
  • Materials per modelGiving one model built from a glTF file materials of its own, while the others built from it keep sharing the source's

Animation6

  • Driving bonesDriving a character's bones from code: the fox's head turns to a target over its clip, and its tail follows a container
  • Playing clipsA glTF model's clips played with AnimationPlayer: one at a time or blended, looped or once, at any speed, or frozen at a time
  • CrowdA crowd of animated characters from one file, each with its own pose, player and place in its clip
  • Tweening with GSAPGSAP tweens a node's position, rotation and scale, and a material's colour
  • KeyframesKeyframe animation built in code: the PixiJS logo assembles itself from tracks an AnimationPlayer plays
  • Morph targets from glTFA glTF file's morph targets, played by its clip and set by hand

Sprites1

  • Sprites in 3DSprites in 3D: a Sprite3D is a picture on a flat quad, sized from its texture and turned toward the camera

Instancing4

  • BatchedMesh3DBatchedMesh3D: one scene node that draws up to 100,000 clay shapes in a few dozen draw calls
  • Batching and cullingThousands of meshes in a handful of draw calls, and the culling that skips what the camera can't see
  • Per-instance dataPer-instance data: every slot of a BatchedMesh3D carries numbers of its own that a shader bit reads, so 18,840 cubes ripple while the CPU writes one number a frame
  • Level of detailLevel of detail in application code: swap a mesh's geometry with its distance from the camera, and keep a band between switches

Particles7

  • Particle systemA particle system: one options object that sets how many sparks are born each second, where, how they fly and how they look
  • Particle containerParticles you drive yourself: a ParticleContainer3D is a fixed pool of particles drawn in one call, and your own code says where each one is every frame
  • Custom particle behaviourA particle shader bit: motion of your own for a ParticleSystem3D, worked out in the vertex shader
  • Custom emitterA custom emitter shape: sparks that start on the logo's surface, at the edge of a burn eating across it
  • Fire and smokeFlipbook particles, billboard modes and blend modes, on a clay campfire
  • Local and world spaceA particle system's space decides whether its particles ride with the node or stay where they were born
  • Bursts on demandBursts on demand: spawn() at a tap, and a seeded burst timeline that stops, clears, replays and scrubs

Interaction7

  • Stamp, fade, reuseStamp marks where the pointer hits a surface, from a pool of six planes that fade and get reused
  • Dragging objectsDrag clay shapes across a plane with globalpointermove and pointerupoutside
  • Event propagationA press travels through the scene graph: in through each container to the node it's for, then back out to the View
  • Hit volumesHit volumes: the shape the pointer is tested against, which needn't be the mesh you see
  • Painting on a meshPainting on a mesh, at the point the pointer touches it
  • Pointer eventsA mesh hears the pointer the way a pixi Sprite does
  • RaycastPicking from your own loop: view3d.raycast once a frame, filling one reused hit

Post-processing15

  • Anti-aliasingAnti-aliasing: MSAA from the canvas's antialias option, which the scene keeps through post effects, and FxaaEffect for a canvas without it
  • BloomBloom: a post effect that makes light brighter than white glow out past its edges
  • Custom effectWriting post effects of your own: a vignette from one fragment, and an edge blur built from three passes
  • Custom mesh passA MeshPass of your own: an x-ray that draws chosen meshes again, in one flat colour, through the wall in front
  • Custom tone mappingOwn the whole output transform: shade into a floating-point target, then tone map and encode in your own effect.
  • Depth of fieldDepth of field: a post effect of your own keeps one camera depth sharp and blurs what's nearer or farther
  • Stacking effectsStacking effects: a view's post effects are one list, view3d.passes, and the frame runs through it in order
  • Outlines and wireframesOutlines and wireframes: passes a material joins by name
  • Motion blurMotion blur: MotionBlurEffect streaks each pixel along the way its surface moved on screen as the camera moved
  • Passes per meshPasses per mesh: one mesh joins or leaves a render pass on its own, whatever its material says
  • PortalsA texture portal: a second camera draws the far side into a render texture, and the opening shows it
  • Render graphThe render graph: a view draws each frame by running a list of passes, and you add steps to it in two ways
  • Scene outputsScene outputs: the scene draw can write more than its colour, each extra texture in the same draw that shades it
  • Ambient occlusionAmbient occlusion: SsaoEffect darkens creases and contact points after the scene is drawn
  • Tilt shiftTilt shift: a blur that keeps one slab of depth sharp, so a full-size scene reads as a miniature

Debug2

  • Seeing the frameSeeing the frame: GraphDebugOverlay records and reviews a view's frames, and RenderGraphDebugger captures one as data
  • Geometry thumbnailsSmall pictures of geometry for an asset browser, with GeometryThumbnailGenerator
